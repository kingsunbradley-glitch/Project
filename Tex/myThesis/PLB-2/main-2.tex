\begin{abstract}
Two complete decay chains assigned to $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ were observed using the gas-filled recoil separator SHANS-2 (Spectrometer for Heavy Atoms and Nuclear Structure-2) in the $^{238}\mathrm{U}(^{40}\mathrm{Ar},5n)^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ reaction, yielding a production cross section of $0.14^{+0.19}_{-0.10}$~pb at $\bar{E}^{*}_{\mathrm{CN}} = 50$~MeV. While one chain was consistent with the known short-lived $\alpha$-decay branch of $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$, the other began with a $10.858(22)$~MeV $\alpha$ decay occurring $8.320$~ms after implantation, followed $9.363$~s later by an $8.916(21)$~MeV $\alpha$ decay of $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$. This decay sequence provides independent evidence for a longer-lived branch in $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ and directly links it to the low-energy $\alpha$ group in $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$, and they are discussed by comparing with the literature data as well as the theoretical prediction.
%  Comparison with configuration-constrained calculations suggests that the longer- and shorter-lived branches in $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ originate from the $\nu\,13/2^{-}[716]$ and $\nu\,3/2^{+}[611]$ configurations, respectively, located one neutron above the deformed $N=162$ subshell.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{introduction}

The enhanced stability of superheavy nuclei arising from quantum shell effects is a central theme in nuclear physics. In particular, the deformed neutron shell gaps at $N=152$ and $N=162$ play a decisive role in stabilizing the heaviest actinide and transactinide nuclei, with $^{252}$Fm~\cite{Orlandi2026} and $^{270}$Hs~\cite{dvorakDoublyMagicNucleus2006} generally recognized as deformed doubly magic nuclei. Extensive experimental progress has been made around $N=152$ through decay spectroscopy~\cite{Ghiorso_1954,Wang_2021,Chen_2025,Zhong-Zhou_2003}, high-precision mass measurements~\cite{Block2010Nature,PhysRevC.87.054324,MinayaRamirez2012DirectMapping}, and in-beam or decay studies of collective and single-particle excitations~\cite{Makii_2007,Sobiczewski_2001}. In contrast, experimental data of the low-lying level structure near \(N=162\) remains limited, particularly for odd-\(A\) nuclei, owing to their low production yields and the experimental challenges associated with decay spectroscopy.

Theoretically, macroscopic--microscopic models employing a deformed Woods--Saxon potential and higher-order deformation degrees of freedom predicted a region of enhanced stability around $Z=108$ and $N=162$ well before its experimental verification~\cite{patyk1989PotentialEnergySpontaneousfission,sobiczewski1989DeformedSuperheavyNuclei}. Subsequent $\alpha$-decay measurements confirmed the stabilizing effect of the $N=162$ shell gap~\cite{hofmann2002NewResultsElements}. While the deformed Woods--Saxon framework successfully reproduces the global ground-state properties in this region, experimental constraints on the ordering and configurations of individual neutron orbitals near $N=162$ are still severely limited. Odd-$A$ nuclei in the immediate vicinity of the shell gap offer a direct probe of these orbitals. Specifically, $^{273}_{110}\mathrm{Ds}_{163}$ and its $\alpha$-decay daughter $^{269}_{108}\mathrm{Hs}_{161}$ correspond to one-neutron-particle and one-neutron-hole systems relative to the $N=162$ subshell closure, providing a sensitive testing ground for single-particle structure predictions.

The isotope $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ was first identified at the GSI velocity filter SHIP as the $\alpha$-decay daughter of $^{277}\mathrm{Cn}$ produced in the $^{208}\mathrm{Pb}(^{70}\mathrm{Zn}, n)$ cold-fusion reaction~\cite{hofmann1996NewElement112,hofmann2002NewResultsElements}, and was later confirmed at RIKEN~\cite{morita2007ExperimentSynthesisIsotope277,sumita2013NewResultProduction}. In all these cold-fusion experiments, the observed decay times of $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ were consistently on the order of $0.1~\si{ms}$. More recently, direct production of $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ via the $^{238}\mathrm{U}(^{40}\mathrm{Ar}, 5n)$ hot-fusion reaction was reported at DGFRS-2 by Oganessian \textit{et al.}~\cite{oganessian2024SynthesisDecayProperties}, where two correlated decay chains were identified. One chain was consistent with the short-lived decay observed in cold-fusion experiments, whereas the other exhibited a substantially longer decay time, suggesting the population of a distinct nuclear state in \(^{273}\mathrm{Ds}\). However, because both chains were incomplete—missing the full-energy $\alpha$ decay of $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$ in the longer-lived branch—the correlation between the $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ states and the subsequent decay pathways of $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$ remained weakly constrained.

In the present work, we used the gas-filled recoil separator SHANS-2~\cite{xu2023GasfilledRecoilSeparator} to investigate the $^{238}\mathrm{U}(^{40}\mathrm{Ar}, 4n\text{--}5n)^{274,273}\mathrm{Ds}$ reaction. Two complete decay chains of $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ were observed. These events corroborate the existence of two distinct decay branches in $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ and establish their genetic links to the subsequent decays of $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$, $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}$, and $^{261}\mathrm{Rf}$, providing new insight into the low-lying quasiparticle structure around $N=162$.

\section{Experiment and theoretical method}

The experiment was performed at the China Accelerator Facility for Superheavy Elements (CAFE2)~\cite{sheng2021IonopticalDesignMultiparticle} of the Institute of Modern Physics by using the gas-filled recoil separator Spectrometer for Heavy Atoms and Nuclear Structure-2 (SHANS-2)~\cite{xu2023GasfilledRecoilSeparator}. The nucleus $^{273}\text{Ds}$ was synthesized in the $^{238}\text{U}(^{40}\text{Ar}, 5n)$ fusion-evaporation reaction. A beam of $^{40}\text{Ar}^{13+}$ at energies of 217, 220, 225 and 227 MeV in the laboratory frame was provided by CAFE2. The typical beam intensity on target was about $5\ \mathrm{p}\mu\mathrm{A}$, and the total effective irradiation time was about 655.2 h.

The targets were prepared by magnetron sputtering of $^{238}\text{U}$ onto Ti backings with thicknesses of 2.22 and 2.19 $\mu\text{g/cm}^{2}$, resulting in average $^{238}\text{U}$ layer thicknesses of 470 and 604 $\mu\text{g/cm}^{2}$. Ten arc-shaped target segments were mounted on a wheel with a diameter of 50 cm and rotated at 1500 rpm during irradiation in order to withstand the high beam load.

Evaporation residues were separated in flight from the primary beam and other reaction products by SHANS-2. The separator was filled with helium gas at a pressure of about 1 mbar, and the magnetic rigidity was optimized for the transmission of $^{273}\text{Ds}$. At the focal plane, the evaporation residues passed through two multi-wire proportional chambers (MWPCs) and were then implanted into a double-sided silicon strip detector (DSSD). The MWPCs were mounted about 20 cm upstream of the DSSD and operated with isobutane gas. The MWPC signals, together with the time-of-flight information, were used to distinguish implanted evaporation residues from subsequent decay events.
The DSSD has an active area of $128 \times 48\ \text{mm}^{2}$ and a thickness of 300 $\mu\text{m}$. The back side, defined as the $X$ plane, is segmented into 128 vertical strips, whereas the front side, defined as the $Y$ plane, is segmented into 48 horizontal strips. This geometry provides 6144 pixels. Six single-sided silicon detectors(SSDs) were arranged upstream of the DSSD in a box-like geometry to detect $\alpha$ particles and spontaneous-fission fragments escaping from the front side of the DSSD. Each detector has a thickness of 500 $\mu\text{m}$ and an active area of $120 \times 63\ \text{mm}^{2}$. In addition, three silicon detectors with a thickness of 300 $\mu\text{m}$ were installed directly behind the DSSD as veto detectors in order to reject light particles punching through the focal-plane detector system. The entire silicon array was cooled to about $-30^{\circ}\text{C}$ with a circulating alcohol system to suppress thermal noise and improve the energy resolution. For full-energy $\alpha$ particles in the range from 5 to 9 MeV, the typical full width at half maximum of the DSSD was about 30 keV. The total detection efficiency of the silicon detector array for $\alpha$ particles was about 87(6)\%. All detector signals were preamplified, shaped, and digitized with 14-bit analog-to-digital converters operated at 100 MHz in a self-triggered mode. Further details of SHANS-2 and the detector system can be found in Refs.~\cite{xu2023GasfilledRecoilSeparator,sheng2021IonopticalDesignMultiparticle}.

The silicon detectors were calibrated with standard $\alpha$ sources
(\ce{^{239}Pu}, \ce{^{241}Am}, and \ce{^{244}Cm}),
and with the well-known $\alpha$-decay lines of
\ce{^{218}Rn}, \ce{^{214}Po}, \ce{^{216}Po}, and \ce{^{217}At}.
The reference $\alpha$-particle energies were adopted from
the National Nuclear Data Center (NNDC) NuDat database
\cite{NNDCNuDat}.

To interpret the observed decay pattern, configuration-constrained potential-energy-surface (PES) calculations were performed in the deformation space $(\beta_2,\beta_4,\beta_6)$. The nuclear surface is parameterized in terms of spherical harmonics, and the total energy is evaluated within the macroscopic--microscopic Strutinsky framework using a realistic phenomenological mean-field Hamiltonian~\cite{brack1972FunnyHillsShellCorrection,xu1998MultiquasiparticlePotentialenergySurfaces,meng2022LandscapeAppreciationSystematic}. In this approach, the total energy consists of a macroscopic contribution described by the standard liquid-drop model~\cite{myersNUCLEARMASSESDEFORMATIONS} and microscopic corrections arising from shell effects~\cite{strutinsky1967ShellEffectsNuclear} and pairing correlations. The single-particle levels are generated from a deformed Woods--Saxon potential with the universal parameter set~\cite{CWIOK1987379}, while pairing correlations are treated using the approximate particle-number-conserving Lipkin--Nogami method~\cite{PRADHAN1973357}, with the monopole pairing strengths $G$ determined from the average-gap prescription~\cite{MOLLER199220}. For each specified one-quasineutron configuration, the corresponding unpaired neutron orbital is continuously tracked and blocked over the entire deformation lattice. The potential-energy surface is then obtained by minimizing the energy with respect to deformation while maintaining the assigned quasiparticle configuration, thereby allowing deformation and pairing effects to be treated self-consistently for each configuration.

The energy and correlation time of the first $\alpha$ decay ($\alpha_1$) in Chain~1 are consistent with those reported in previous $^{208}\mathrm{Pb}(^{70}\mathrm{Zn},n)^{277}\mathrm{Cn}$ experiments at GSI \cite{hofmann1996NewElement112,hofmann2002NewResultsElements} and RIKEN \cite{morita2007ExperimentSynthesisIsotope277,sumita2013NewResultProduction}. To assign the subsequent signals ($\alpha_2$, $\alpha_3$, and terminal spontaneous fission), we examine the established decay pathways along the $^{269}\mathrm{Hs} \rightarrow {}^{265}\mathrm{Sg}^{a,b} \rightarrow {}^{261}\mathrm{Rf}^{a,b}$ chain \cite{hofmann2002NewResultsElements,dvorakDoublyMagicNucleus2006,dullmannCm248Ne2008,dvorakObservation3Evaporation2008,habaProductionDecayProperties2011,habaProduction265Sg2012}.

Two states are known in both $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}$ and $^{261}\mathrm{Rf}$ \cite{habaProductionDecayProperties2011,habaProduction265Sg2012}. In $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}$, the state $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}^{a}$ ($T_{1/2} = 8.5^{+2.6}_{-1.6}$~s, $E_{\alpha} = 8.84 \pm 0.05$~MeV) abundantly (91\%) populates the longer-lived $^{261}\mathrm{Rf}^{a}$ ($T_{1/2} = 68 \pm 3$~s), which decays predominantly by $\alpha$ emission ($E_{\alpha} = 8.28$~MeV). Conversely, $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}^{b}$ ($T_{1/2} = 14.4^{+3.7}_{-2.5}$~s, $E_{\alpha} = 8.69 \pm 0.05$~MeV) preferentially (80\%) populates the shorter-lived $^{261}\mathrm{Rf}^{b}$ ($T_{1/2} = 2.6^{+0.7}_{-0.5}$~s), which decays mainly via spontaneous fission ($b_{\mathrm{SF}} = 82 \pm 9\%$) \cite{habaProduction265Sg2012}. Both the $\alpha_3$ decay properties and the subsequent terminal spontaneous-fission events observed in Chains~1 and~2 agree well with the $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}^{b} \rightarrow {}^{261}\mathrm{Rf}^{b}$ branch, firmly establishing this pathway for both chains.

Feeding into $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}$, previous studies of $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$ reported two $\alpha$-particle energy groups at approximately $9.13$ and $8.95$~MeV \cite{vonzweidorfEvidenceFormationSodium2004,turlerDecayProperties269Hs2003,dvorakDoublyMagicNucleus2006,dvorakObservation3Evaporation2008,turlerNuclearStructureReaction2012}, which match the measured $\alpha_2$ energies in Chain~1 and Chain~2, respectively. The observation of two distinct $\alpha_2$ energy groups followed by similar decay properties characteristic of $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}^{b}$ suggests a complex decay pattern in $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$, potentially involving low-lying nuclear states, as discussed below.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Decay properties of~$^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$}{Decay properties of 273Ds}}

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:EalphaAndDecaytime}, most events in the two chains observed in the present work agree well with previously reported data. The striking difference in $\alpha$-decay energies between $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ and $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$ is consistent with the stabilizing effect of the $N=162$ deformed shell gap. A notable exception is one $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ decay event with $E_{\alpha} = 10.858(22)\ \mathrm{MeV}$ and $\Delta t = 8.320\ \mathrm{ms}$, which differs substantially from the higher-energy, shorter-lived decays observed at GSI and RIKEN. Based on the reported half-life of $T_{1/2}=0.18^{+0.11}_{-0.05}\ \mathrm{ms}$ for the short-lived state \cite{oganessian2024SynthesisDecayProperties}, the probability of observing a decay time of $\Delta t>5\ \mathrm{ms}$ is only $P=4.3\times10^{-9}$. This strongly suggests that the observed event originates from a different state in $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$. Combined with the decay event reported in Ref.~\cite{oganessian2024SynthesisDecayProperties} ($E_{\alpha} = 10.929(22)\ \mathrm{MeV}$ and $\Delta t = 41.703\ \mathrm{ms}$), the present observation supports the existence of a lower-energy, longer-lived branch in $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$. Nevertheless, it remains uncertain whether these two events stem from the same nuclear state. Given the incomplete daughter-decay information for the JINR event and the difference in their measured $\alpha$-particle energies, we tentatively assign them to distinct states.

The nucleus $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ has been populated via two pathways: as the $\alpha$-decay daughter of $^{277}\mathrm{Cn}$ in the $^{208}\mathrm{Pb}(^{70}\mathrm{Zn}, n)$ cold-fusion reaction at GSI~\cite{hofmann2002NewResultsElements,hofmann1996NewElement112} and RIKEN~\cite{sumita2013NewResultProduction,morita2007ExperimentSynthesisIsotope277}, and by direct production in the $^{238}\mathrm{U}(^{40}\mathrm{Ar}, 5n)$ hot-fusion reaction at JINR~\cite{oganessian2024SynthesisDecayProperties} and in the present work. Two distinct groups of decay properties have now been identified. To distinguish from the $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{a,b}$ notation used in Ref.~\cite{oganessian2024SynthesisDecayProperties}, we denote the lower-energy, longer-lived branch as $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{l}$ and the higher-energy, shorter-lived branch as $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{h}$. The available data indicate that feeding from the $\alpha$ decay of $^{277}\mathrm{Cn}$ selectively populates $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{h}$, whereas direct evaporation-residue production populates both branches. This population pattern is consistent with the assignment of $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{l}$ to a distinct low-lying isomeric or ground state.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Decay properties of~$^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$}{Decay properties of 269Hs}}

T\"urler \textit{et al.}~\cite{turlerNuclearStructureReaction2012} pointed out that the reported $\alpha$-decay energies of $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$ in the range 9.17--9.25 MeV, observed as descendants of $^{277}\mathrm{Cn}$ and $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$, are among the highest known for this nuclide. Two possible explanations were mentioned: energy summing with conversion electrons or the presence of long-lived states in $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$, similar to those established in $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}$ and $^{261}\mathrm{Rf}$. However, the limited energy resolution and relatively large uncertainties of some previous measurements make it difficult to distinguish closely spaced $\alpha$-decay branches. Therefore, rather than adopting specific state assignments from previous studies, we classify the compiled $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$ events into two empirical energy groups, denoted as $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{h}$ and $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{l}$ for the high- and low-energy groups, respectively, using $E_\alpha=9.0$ MeV as a practical dividing line.
%These labels do not necessarily imply the existence of two distinct nuclear states. 
The event observed in the present work, with $E_\alpha=8.916(21)$ MeV and $\Delta t=9.363$ s, is assigned to $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{l}$ and represents the first lifetime determination for this energy branch, completing the relevant whole decay chain.

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:distributionHs}, both $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{h}$ and $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{l}$ are populated in the directly produced $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$ sample. The corresponding event ratios are $n(^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{h}):n(^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{l})=11:5$ for the directly produced sample and $6:1$ for the sample populated through the $\alpha$ decay of $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$. These ratios are based on the full event compilation shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:distributionHs}, including the literature event with an uncertain assignment. Notably, the $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{l}$ event populated through $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ decay is associated with its longer-lived branch, whereas the $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{h}$ events are predominantly associated with the shorter-lived branch. No clear evidence for the cross-population channels $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{h}\rightarrow{}^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{l}$ or $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{l}\rightarrow{}^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{h}$ is found in the available data. Because $\alpha$-decay transitions are sensitive to the initial and final states of the parent and daughter nuclei, favored decays preferentially connect states with similar configurations for comparable $Q_{\alpha}$ values~\cite{asai2015NuclearStructureElements}. Although the limited statistics prevent a definitive conclusion, this apparent selectivity may indicate the population of different low-lying configurations in $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$ through the two $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ decay branches.

It should be noted that the $\alpha$-particle energies reported for the deformed odd-$A$ nuclei along the decay chain of $^{277}\mathrm{Cn}$ exhibit considerable variations, in some cases spanning several hundred keV \cite{dullmannCm248Ne2008,habaProduction265Sg2012}. Such variations may reflect a complex level structure involving low-lying one-quasiparticle states and their associated rotational bands, which can be populated through different $\alpha$-decay transitions and subsequently deexcite via electromagnetic transitions. In addition, finite detector energy resolution and possible summing effects involving conversion electrons may contribute to the observed energy distributions. Consequently, the measured $\alpha$-particle energies alone do not allow an unambiguous determination of the underlying nuclear configurations and decay pathways. Detailed decay studies by $\alpha$--$\gamma$ and $\alpha$--electron coincidence spectroscopy are therefore desirable. Taking into account the observed $\alpha$-decay energies, decay times, subsequent decay branches, and available theoretical predictions, we tentatively propose the spin-parity assignments and decay scheme shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:energylevel}.

Neutron one-quasiparticle states in $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ have been investigated within macroscopic-microscopic models \cite{jachimowicz2014QalphaValuesSuperheavy,jachimowicz2025CandidatesThreequasiparticleIsomers,cwiok1994ShellStructureHeaviest} and the Hartree-Fock-Bogoliubov framework \cite{Berger2003Gogny,nazarewiczTheoreticalDescriptionSuperheavy}. These calculations identify the $\nu13/2^-[716]$, $\nu3/2^+[611]$, and $\nu5/2^+[613]$ configurations as low-lying candidates. In particular, the $\nu13/2^-[716]$ configuration is favored as the ground state in several calculations \cite{moller2019NuclearPropertiesAstrophysical,jachimowicz2025CandidatesThreequasiparticleIsomers,Berger2003Gogny,cwiok1994ShellStructureHeaviest}, consistent with our results. Furthermore, both our calculations and Refs.~\cite{Berger2003Gogny,cwiok1994ShellStructureHeaviest} favor the $\nu3/2^+[611]$ configuration as the first excited state. A direct electromagnetic transition from the $\nu3/2^+[611]$ state to the $\nu13/2^-[716]$ ground state would require a high multipolarity, with E5 being the lowest allowed multipolarity. Such a transition is expected to be strongly hindered, making the $\nu3/2^+[611]$ state a plausible spin-isomer candidate. Accordingly, we tentatively assign the $\nu13/2^-[716]$ and $\nu3/2^+[611]$ configurations to the long-lived $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{a}$ and short-lived $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{b}$ states, respectively.

For the parent nucleus $^{277}\mathrm{Cn}$, our calculations favor the $\nu3/2^+[611]$ configuration as the ground state, consistent with the spin-parity assignments predicted in Refs.~\cite{moller2019NuclearPropertiesAstrophysical,jachimowicz2025CandidatesThreequasiparticleIsomers}. This assignment provides a natural explanation for the preferential population of $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{b}$ in the decay chains originating from $^{277}\mathrm{Cn}$, since the transition between the corresponding $\nu3/2^+[611]$ configurations is expected to be favored, whereas direct population of the $\nu13/2^-[716]$ configuration would involve a substantial change in angular momentum and parity.

% HF 

To establish the configurations of the daughter nuclei, we next consider the neutron one-quasiparticle states in the region between the deformed subshell closures at $N=152$ and $N=162$. Calculations predict that the $\nu9/2^+[615]$, $\nu7/2^+[613]$, $\nu3/2^+[622]$, $\nu1/2^+[620]$, and $\nu11/2^-[725]$ orbitals are important candidates for low-lying configurations in $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$, $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}$, and $^{261}\mathrm{Rf}$ \cite{Parkhomenko2005,cwiok1994ShellStructureHeaviest,nazarewiczTheoreticalDescriptionSuperheavy}. However, the relative ordering of these orbitals remains model dependent, making the identification of the ground and isomeric states uncertain.

For the $N=157$ nucleus $^{261}\mathrm{Rf}$, we tentatively assign the $\nu9/2^+[615]$ configuration to the long-lived $^{261}\mathrm{Rf}^{a}$ state as ground state. This assignment is supported by the experimental systematics of neighboring $N=157$ isotones, for which the $\nu9/2^+[615]$ ground-state configuration has been established in $^{259}\mathrm{No}$ and $^{257}\mathrm{Fm}$ \cite{asai2013GroundstateConfiguration157}. The $\nu3/2^+[622]$ configuration is then considered a candidate for the short-lived $^{261}\mathrm{Rf}^{b}$ state. The large spontaneous-fission branching ratio of $^{261}\mathrm{Rf}^{b}$, $b_{\mathrm{SF}}=0.82\pm0.09$ \cite{habaProduction265Sg2012}, is qualitatively compatible with a configuration exhibiting weaker fission hindrance.% 这与计算的HF相符

For $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}$, both our calculations and previous theoretical studies identify the $\nu3/2^+[622]$ configuration as a plausible ground-state candidate, while the $\nu9/2^+[615]$ configuration may form a low-lying excited state \cite{oganessian2024SynthesisDecayProperties,malovElectromagneticTransitionsLowlying2022}. The hindrance factors, defined as the ratio of experimental to theoretical $\alpha$-decay partial half-lives, provide additional constraints. Assuming an $\alpha$-decay branching ratio of unity, the estimated HF values for the main $\alpha$ branches of $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}^{a}$ are 2.12 \cite{oganessianMeasurementsCrossSections2004} and 2.87~\cite{Poenaru1980,Rurarz1983}, while those of $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}^{b}$ are 1.35~\cite{oganessianMeasurementsCrossSections2004} and 1.82~\cite{Poenaru1980,Rurarz1983}. These values indicate relatively weak apparent hindrance and are compatible with transitions between similar neutron configurations. We therefore tentatively assign the $\nu9/2^+[615]$ configuration to $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}^{a}$ and the $\nu3/2^+[622]$ configuration to $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}^{b}$, corresponding to an excited high-$K$ state and the ground state, respectively. 

For $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$, several theoretical approaches favor the $\nu9/2^+[615]$ configuration as the ground state~\cite{Parkhomenko2005,jachimowicz2025CandidatesThreequasiparticleIsomers,Berger2003Gogny,malovElectromagneticTransitionsLowlying2022,oganessian2024SynthesisDecayProperties}, while the $\nu3/2^+[622]$ orbital is also predicted among the possible excited configurations \cite{Parkhomenko2005,malovElectromagneticTransitionsLowlying2022}. The calculated excitation energies and level ordering are nevertheless model dependent. The estimated HF values are 0.678~\cite{oganessianMeasurementsCrossSections2004} and 0.843~\cite{Poenaru1980,Rurarz1983} for the lower-energy $\alpha$ branch, $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{l}$, and 3.68~\cite{oganessianMeasurementsCrossSections2004} and 4.51~\cite{Poenaru1980,Rurarz1983} for the higher-energy branch, $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{h}$. Both sets of values are consistent with relatively weakly hindered $\alpha$ transitions. Combining these observations with the proposed configurations in $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}$, we tentatively assign $\nu9/2^+[615]$ to the $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{l}$ ground state and $\nu3/2^+[622]$ to the $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{h}$ excited state. The corresponding configuration-preserving transitions would preferentially populate the $\nu9/2^+[615]$ state in $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}^{a}$ and the $\nu3/2^+[622]$ state in $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}^{b}$, respectively.

The frequent observation of the $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}^{b}$ decay branch following $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{l}$ may involve an additional electromagnetic transition in $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}$. In this interpretation, the $\alpha$ decay of $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{l}$ first populates the excited $\nu9/2^+[615]$ configuration in $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}^{a}$, which may subsequently decay electromagnetically to the lower-lying $\nu3/2^+[622]$ state before further $\alpha$ decay. However, such an electromagnetic branch has not been directly observed, and its competition with $\alpha$ decay remains to be established. Consequently, this pathway is included only as a tentative possibility in the proposed decay scheme.

With these assignments, the two observed $\alpha$-decay branches of $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ can be examined in terms of their different hindrance factors. The long-lived $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{a}$ branch exhibits relatively large HF values of approximately 27.1~\cite{Poenaru1980,Rurarz1983} and 28.2~\cite{oganessianMeasurementsCrossSections2004}, whereas the short-lived $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{b}$ branch has much smaller values of approximately 3.86~\cite{Poenaru1980,Rurarz1983} and 4.21~\cite{oganessianMeasurementsCrossSections2004}. For $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{a}$, assigned to the $\nu13/2^-[716]$ configuration, the $\nu11/2^-[725]$ orbital is the only negative-parity state among the considered low-lying candidates in $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$. The $\nu13/2^-[716]\rightarrow\nu11/2^-[725]$ transition therefore preserves parity, making it a plausible decay pathway. This interpretation is also consistent with the calculated hindrance factors, indicating a moderately hindered $\alpha$ transition. We therefore tentatively favor the population of the $\nu11/2^-[725]$ state in the $\alpha$ decay of $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{a}$.

The relatively small hindrance factors of $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{b}$, approximately 4, suggest a weakly hindered $\alpha$ transition. The direct $\nu3/2^+[611]\xrightarrow{\alpha}\nu3/2^+[622]$ transition involves a reversal of the intrinsic spin projection, which is expected to result in substantial $\alpha$-decay hindrance. In contrast, the $\nu3/2^+[611]$ and $\nu1/2^+[620]$ orbitals have the same intrinsic spin projection and can be associated with the same spherical $2g_{7/2}$ subshell in certain Nilsson descriptions, suggesting a potentially more favorable structural overlap. We therefore propose that the $\alpha$ decay of $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{b}$ may preferentially populate the $I^\pi=3/2^+$ member of the rotational band built on the $\nu1/2^+[620]$ configuration in $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$, followed by electromagnetic deexcitation to the $\nu3/2^+[622]$ state. Such indirect feeding through excited rotational-band states has been discussed in Ref.~\cite{ackermann2024IsomersSuperheavyNuclei}. An experimental example of $\alpha$-decay feeding of an excited daughter state followed by electromagnetic deexcitation was reported in $^{257}\mathrm{Rf}$, where the $\alpha$ decay populates the $\nu11/2^-[725]$ state in $^{253}\mathrm{No}$, which subsequently decays to the $\nu9/2^-[734]$ ground state through an M1 transition \cite{Hauschild2022}. 

The resulting decay scheme therefore represents a tentative interpretation that combines the available experimental decay properties with theoretical one-quasiparticle configurations and possible electromagnetic feeding paths. While the proposed assignments provide a consistent framework for discussing the different $\alpha$-decay branches, they are not unique. Future $\alpha$--$\gamma$ and $\alpha$--electron coincidence measurements, together with more detailed calculations of rotational-band structures, electromagnetic transition rates, and $\alpha$-decay hindrance factors, will be essential for establishing the underlying configurations and decay pathways.

The excitation-function information provides an additional consistency check for the present interpretation. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:exFun}, the measured cross section at the present beam energy is broadly consistent with the calculated expectation that the $5n$ channel dominates near this energy. This agreement supports the assignment of the observed chains to $^{273}$Ds produced in the $^{238}$U($^{40}$Ar, $5n$)$^{273}$Ds reaction rather than to neighboring evaporation channels. Although the excitation function does not by itself distinguish between different intrinsic states, it shows that the long-lived and short-lived branches emerge within the same reaction framework. This point strengthens the conclusion that the difference between the two branches is more likely to be structural than to originate from different reaction channels.


\section{Summary and conclusions}

Two complete decay chains of $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ were directly produced at SHANS-2 at a compound-nucleus excitation energy of $50$~MeV. The resulting $5n$ cross section is consistent within uncertainties with the previous DGFRS-2 measurement~\cite{oganessian2024SynthesisDecayProperties}, while cross-section upper limits of $0.32$ and $1.1$~pb were obtained at $42$ and $45$~MeV, respectively.

The observed chains map both the higher- and lower-energy $\alpha$ decays of $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ through $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$: the $11.165(22)$~MeV short-lived transition was followed by a $9.103(22)$~MeV $\alpha$ decay of $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$, whereas the longer-lived branch was correlated with an $8.916(21)$~MeV daughter decay---a link missing from the earlier observation~\cite{oganessian2024SynthesisDecayProperties}. Both chains terminate via a $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}$ $\alpha$ decay and subsequent fission event, consistent with the $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}^{b}\rightarrow{}^{261}\mathrm{Rf}^{b}$ sequence.

Combining the decay properties with calculated one-quasineutron levels favors a $\nu\,13/2^{-}[716]$ ground state and a low-lying $\nu\,3/2^{+}[611]$ state in $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$. In this picture, the shorter-lived branch decays from a candidate spin isomer, as a direct electromagnetic transition to the ground-state bandhead has a lowest allowed multipolarity of $E5$. For $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$, the two $\alpha$-energy groups could reflect either distinct parent states or transitions from a single state to different levels in $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}$. Distinguishing between these scenarios will require $\alpha$--$\gamma$ and $\alpha$--electron coincidence measurements to identify the populated daughter levels in each branch.
