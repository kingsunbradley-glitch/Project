\title{\texorpdfstring{Probing the deformed $N=162$ subshell by the $^{238}$U($^{40}$Ar , $5n$)$^{273}$Ds reaction}{Probing the deformed N=162 subshell by the 238U(40Ar, 5n)273Ds reaction}}

\author{Z.\ R.\ Sun \hide{()}}
\affiliation{State Key Laboratory of Heavy Ion Science and Technology, Institute of Modern Physics, Chinese Academy of Sciences, Lanzhou 730000, China}
\affiliation{School of Nuclear Science and Technology, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100049, China}

\begin{abstract}
A series of experiments was carried out at the SHANS-2 separator of IMP CAS to study the $^{238}$U($^{40}$Ar, $4n$--$5n$)$^{274,273}$Ds reaction. The excitation function was investigated in the excitation-energy range from 42 to 50 MeV. At 42 and 45 MeV, no correlated decay chain attributable to $^{273}$Ds was identified, and upper limits of 0.34 and 1.09 pb were obtained. At 50 MeV, two complete decay chains of $^{273}$Ds were observed, corresponding to a cross section of $0.14^{+0.18}_{-0.08}$ pb, in agreement with the value of $0.18^{+0.44}_{-0.12}$ pb reported previously for the same reaction at DGFRS-2. One chain follows the known high-energy, short-lived branch, whereas the other defines a lower-energy, longer-lived branch that supports the interpretation of a distinct state in $^{273}$Ds. By combining the present data with earlier results, the population pattern and the $\alpha$-particle energy distribution of $^{269}$Hs suggest the presence of an additional low-energy state in this nucleus. The combined decay properties and isotopic systematics of Ds and Hs provide new constraints on the single-particle structure around the deformed $N=162$ shell closure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{introduction}

The stability of superheavy nuclei resulting from shell effects is one of the most intriguing subjects in nuclear physics.
In particular, the deformed neutron shell gaps at $N=152$ and $N=162$ play important roles in the stabilization of the heaviest nuclei.
At present, $^{252}$Fm\cite{Orlandi2026} and $^{270}$Hs\cite{dvorakDoublyMagicNucleus2006} are generally regarded as deformed doubly magic nuclei.
Considerable progress has been made in understanding the structure of nuclei around $N=152$ by decay properties \cite{Ghiorso_1954,Wang_2021,Chen_2025,Zhong-Zhou_2003}, mass measurements \cite{Block2010Nature,PhysRevC.87.054324,MinayaRamirez2012DirectMapping}, and spectroscopic studies of collective and single-particle excitations \cite{Makii_2007,Sobiczewski_2001}.
In contrast, experimental knowledge of the low-lying level structure near $N=162$ remains limited, primarily because of the extremely low production cross sections of nuclei in this region.

On the theoretical side, macroscopic--microscopic approaches have played an important role in establishing the shell structure of the superheavy nuclei. In this framework, a smooth macroscopic contribution to the nuclear energy is supplemented by microscopic shell and pairing corrections, with the single-particle spectrum commonly generated using a deformed Woods--Saxon potential. Calculations employing the Woods--Saxon potential together with higher-order deformation degrees of freedom predicted a pronounced region of enhanced stability around $Z=108$ and $N=162$ well before its experimental confirmation \cite{patyk1989PotentialEnergySpontaneousfission,sobiczewski1989DeformedSuperheavyNuclei}. Subsequent $\alpha$-decay studies provided experimental evidence for the enhanced stability associated with the predicted $N=162$ shell gap \cite{lazarevDiscoveryEnhancedNuclear1994}. The successful description of this region makes the deformed Woods--Saxon framework particularly suitable for investigating the underlying single-particle structure around $N=162$. Nevertheless, experimental data on the ordering and configurations of low-lying neutron single-particle states near the $N=162$ subshell remain scarce.

To explore the level structure around the deformed $N=162$ subshell, the $^{238}$U($^{40}$Ar,$5n$)$^{273}$Ds reaction was investigated in the present work. 
Investigation of low-lying single-particle orbitals in odd-A heavy nuclei provides a unique probe to the nuclear structure. Especially, $^{273}_{110}\mathrm{Ds}_{163}$ and its $\alpha$-decay daughter $^{269}_{108}\mathrm{Hs}_{161}$, which represent one particle and one hole systems with respect to the $N=162$ subshell closure, and thus offer direct access to the low-lying neutron single-particle structure around the $N=162$ subshell.
In this work, two complete decay chains were observed and assigned to $^{273}$Ds on the basis of genetic correlations \cite{geneticcorrelations} and their consistency with previously reported decay properties. 

The isotope $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ was first identified at the GSI velocity filter SHIP as an $\alpha$-decay daughter in cold-fusion reactions \cite{hofmann2002NewResultsElements}. 
In the $^{208}\mathrm{Pb}(^{70}\mathrm{Zn}, n)^{277}\mathrm{Cn}$ cold-fusion experiments at GSI \cite{hofmann2002NewResultsElements,hofmann1996NewElement112} and RIKEN \cite{sumita2013NewResultProduction,morita2007ExperimentSynthesisIsotope277}, the decay times attributed to $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ were consistently of the order of $0.1~\si{ms}$. 
More recently, direct production of $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ through the $^{238}\mathrm{U}(^{40}\mathrm{Ar}, 5n)$ reaction was reported by Oganessian \textit{et al.}~\cite{oganessian2024SynthesisDecayProperties}. 
Two correlated decay chains were identified \cite{geneticcorrelations}. 
One chain agreed with previous data, whereas the other was assigned to an isomeric state of $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$. 
Because both chains were incomplete, the decay properties of the daughter nucleus $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$ remained only weakly constrained.
%Subsequent theoretical analyses based on the DDCM+ and UDF approaches led to minor proposed revisions of the assigned decay paths \cite{Wang2024}. 
In the present work, the same reaction was studied at the SHANS-2 \cite{xu2023GasfilledRecoilSeparator}, and two additional complete decay chains assigned to $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ were observed. 
These observations provide additional evidence for distinct decay patterns in $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ and establish further correlations with the subsequent decays of $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$, offering new constraints on the low-lying nuclear structure near the $N=162$ subshell.

\section{Experiment and Theoretical method}

The experiment was performed at the China Accelerator Facility for Superheavy Elements (CAFE2)\cite{sheng2021IonopticalDesignMultiparticle} of the Institute of Modern Physics by using the gas-filled recoil separator Spectrometer for Heavy Atoms and Nuclear Structure-2(SHANS-2)\cite{xu2023GasfilledRecoilSeparator}. The nucleus $^{273}\text{Ds}$ was synthesized in the $^{238}\text{U}(^{40}\text{Ar}, 5n)$ fusion-evaporation reaction. A beam of $^{40}\text{Ar}^{13+}$ at energies of 217, 220, and 225 MeV in the laboratory frame  was provided by CAFE2. The typical beam intensity on target was about $5\ \mathrm{p}\mu\mathrm{A}$, and the total effective irradiation time was about 728 h.

The targets were prepared by magnetron sputtering of $^{238}\text{U}$ onto Ti backings with thicknesses of 2.22 and 2.19 $\mu\text{g/cm}^{2}$, resulting in average $^{238}\text{U}$ layer thicknesses of 470 and 604 $\mu\text{g/cm}^{2}$. Ten arc-shaped target segments were mounted on a wheel with a diameter of 50 cm and rotated at 1500 rpm during irradiation in order to withstand the high beam load.

Evaporation residues were separated in flight from the primary beam and other reaction products by SHANS2. The separator was filled with helium gas at a pressure of about 1 mbar, and the magnetic rigidity was optimized for the transmission of $^{273}\text{Ds}$. At the focal plane, the evaporation residues passed through two multi-wire proportional chambers (MWPCs) and were then implanted into a double-sided silicon strip detector (DSSD). The MWPCs were mounted about 20 cm upstream of the DSSD and operated with isobutane gas to distinguish the ER implantation from the decay events. The MWPC signals, together with the time-of-flight information, were used to distinguish implanted evaporation residues from subsequent decay events.
The DSSD has an active area of $128 \times 48\ \text{mm}^{2}$ and a thickness of 300 $\mu\text{m}$. The front side, defined as the $X$ plane, is segmented into 128 vertical strips, whereas the back side, defined as the $Y$ plane, is segmented into 48 horizontal strips. This geometry provides 6144 pixels. Six single-sided silicon detectors were arranged upstream of the DSSD in a box-like geometry to detect $\alpha$ particles and spontaneous-fission fragments escaping from the front side of the DSSD. Each detector has a thickness of 500 $\mu\text{m}$ and an active area of $120 \times 63\ \text{mm}^{2}$. In addition, three silicon detectors with a thickness of 300 $\mu\text{m}$ were installed directly behind the DSSD as veto detectors in order to reject light particles punching through the focal-plane detector system. The entire silicon array was cooled to about $-30^{\circ}\text{C}$ with a circulating alcohol system to suppress thermal noise and improve the energy resolution. For full-energy $\alpha$ particles in the range from 5 to 9 MeV, the typical full width at half maximum of the DSSD was about 30 keV. The total detection efficiency of the silicon detector array for $\alpha$ particles was about 87(6)\%. All detector signals were preamplified, shaped, and digitized with 14-bit analog-to-digital converters operated at 100 MHz in a self-triggered mode. Further details of SHANS2 and the detector system can be found in Refs.~\cite{xu2023GasfilledRecoilSeparator,sheng2021IonopticalDesignMultiparticle}.

The silicon detectors were calibrated with standard $\alpha$ sources
(\ce{^{239}Pu}, \ce{^{241}Am}, and \ce{^{244}Cm}),
and with the well-known $\alpha$-decay lines of
\ce{^{218}Rn}, \ce{^{214}Po}, \ce{^{216}Po}, and \ce{^{217}At}.
The reference $\alpha$-particle energies were adopted from
the National Nuclear Data Center (NNDC) NuDat database
\cite{NNDCNuDat}.
The separator transmission efficiency used for the cross-section evaluation was obtained from simulations performed according to the procedure described in Ref.~\cite{zhang2026MonteCarloSimulation}, yielding a value of 20\%. The experimental conditions and the measured cross sections are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:cross_sections}.

To interpret the observed decay pattern, configuration-constrained potential-energy-surface (PES) calculations were performed in the deformation space $(\beta_2,\beta_4,\beta_6)$. The nuclear surface is parameterized in terms of spherical harmonics, and the total energy is evaluated within the macroscopic--microscopic Strutinsky framework using a realistic phenomenological mean-field Hamiltonian~\cite{brack1972FunnyHillsShellCorrection,xu1998MultiquasiparticlePotentialenergySurfaces,meng2022LandscapeAppreciationSystematic}. In this approach, the total energy consists of a macroscopic contribution described by the standard liquid-drop model~\cite{myersNUCLEARMASSESDEFORMATIONS} and microscopic corrections arising from shell effects~\cite{strutinsky1967ShellEffectsNuclear} and pairing correlations. The single-particle levels are generated from a deformed Woods--Saxon potential with the universal parameter set~\cite{CWIOK1987379}, while pairing correlations are treated using the approximate particle-number-conserving Lipkin--Nogami method~\cite{PRADHAN1973357}, with the monopole pairing strengths $G$ determined from the average-gap prescription~\cite{MOLLER199220}. For each specified one-quasineutron configuration, the corresponding unpaired neutron orbital is continuously tracked and blocked over the entire deformation lattice. The potential-energy surface is then obtained by minimizing the energy with respect to deformation while maintaining the assigned quasiparticle configuration, thereby allowing deformation and pairing effects to be treated self-consistently for each configuration.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Decay properties of ~ $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$}{Decay properties of 273Ds}}

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:EalphaAndDecaytime}, most events in the two chains observed in the present work agree well with previously reported data. A notable exception is one $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ decay event with $E_{\alpha} = 10.858(22)\ \mathrm{MeV}$ and $\Delta t = 8.320\ \mathrm{ms}$, which differs substantially from the higher-energy, shorter-lived decays observed at GSI and RIKEN. Based on the reported half-life of $T_{1/2}=0.18^{+0.11}_{-0.05}\ \mathrm{ms}$ for the short-lived state \cite{oganessian2024SynthesisDecayProperties}, the probability of observing a decay time of $\Delta t>5\ \mathrm{ms}$ is only $P=4.3\times10^{-9}$. This strongly suggests that the observed event originates from a different state in $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$. Combined with the decay event reported in Ref.~\cite{oganessian2024SynthesisDecayProperties} [$E_{\alpha} = 10.929(22)\ \mathrm{MeV}$ and $\Delta t = 41.703\ \mathrm{ms}$], the present observation supports the existence of a lower-energy, longer-lived branch in $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$. Nevertheless, whether these two events originate from the exact same state remains uncertain.

The nucleus $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ has been populated via two pathways: as the $\alpha$-decay daughter of $^{277}\mathrm{Cn}$ in the $^{208}\mathrm{Pb}(^{70}\mathrm{Zn}, n)$ cold-fusion reaction at GSI~\cite{hofmann2002NewResultsElements,hofmann1996NewElement112} and RIKEN~\cite{sumita2013NewResultProduction,morita2007ExperimentSynthesisIsotope277}, and by direct production in the $^{238}\mathrm{U}(^{40}\mathrm{Ar}, 5n)$ hot-fusion reaction at JINR~\cite{oganessian2024SynthesisDecayProperties} and in the present work. Two distinct groups of decay properties have now been identified. Following Ref.~\cite{oganessian2024SynthesisDecayProperties}, the lower-energy, longer-lived branch and the higher-energy, shorter-lived branch are denoted as $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{a}$ and $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{b}$, respectively. The available data indicate that feeding from the $\alpha$ decay of $^{277}\mathrm{Cn}$ selectively populates $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{b}$, whereas direct evaporation-residue production populates both branches. This population pattern is consistent with the assignment of $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{a}$ to a distinct low-lying isomeric or ground state.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Decay properties of ~ $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$}{Decay properties of 269Hs}}

T\"urler \textit{et al.}~\cite{turlerNuclearStructureReaction2012} pointed out that the reported $\alpha$-decay energies of $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$ in the range 9.17--9.25 MeV, observed as descendants of $^{277}\mathrm{Cn}$ and $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$, are among the highest known for this nuclide. Two possible explanations were mentioned: energy summing with conversion electrons or the presence of long-lived states in $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$, similar to those established in $^{265}\mathrm{Sg}$ and $^{261}\mathrm{Rf}$. However, the limited energy resolution and relatively large uncertainties of some previous measurements make it difficult to distinguish closely spaced $\alpha$-decay branches. Therefore, rather than adopting specific state assignments from previous studies, we classify the compiled $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$ events into two empirical energy groups, denoted as $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{h}$ and $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{l}$ for the high- and low-energy groups, respectively, using $E_\alpha=9.0$ MeV as a practical dividing line.
%These labels do not necessarily imply the existence of two distinct nuclear states. 
The event observed in the present work, with $E_\alpha=8.915$ MeV and $\tau=9.363$ s, is assigned to $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{l}$ and represents the first lifetime determination for this energy branch.

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:distributionHs}, both $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{h}$ and $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{l}$ are populated in the directly produced $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$ sample. The corresponding event ratios are $n(^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{h}):n(^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{l})=11:5$ for the directly produced sample and $6:1$ for the sample populated through the $\alpha$ decay of $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$. These ratios are based on the full event compilation shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:distributionHs}, including the literature event with an uncertain assignment. Notably, the $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{l}$ event populated through $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ decay is associated with its longer-lived branch, whereas the $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{h}$ events are predominantly associated with the shorter-lived branch. No clear evidence for the cross-population channels $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{b}\rightarrow{}^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{l}$ or $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}^{a}\rightarrow{}^{269}\mathrm{Hs}^{h}$ is found in the available data. Although the limited statistics prevent a definitive conclusion, this apparent selectivity may indicate the population of different low-lying configurations in $^{269}\mathrm{Hs}$ through the two $^{273}\mathrm{Ds}$ decay branches.

\begin{align*}
^{273}\text{Ds}^{b} &\xrightarrow{\alpha} {}^{269}\text{Hs}^{h} \xrightarrow{\alpha} {}^{265}\text{Sg}^{b} \xrightarrow{\alpha} {}^{261}\text{Rf}^{b} \xrightarrow{\text{SF}} \text{fission} .

\begin{align*}
^{273}\text{Ds}^{a} &\xrightarrow{\alpha} {}^{269}\text{Hs}^{l} \xrightarrow{\alpha} {}^{265}\text{Sg}^{b} \xrightarrow{\alpha} {}^{261}\text{Rf}^{b} \xrightarrow{\text{SF}} \text{fission} .
\end{align*}

When the present chains are considered together with the chain reported in Ref.~\cite{oganessian2024SynthesisDecayProperties}, this separation is difficult to reconcile with a single-state interpretation. %A plausible physical interpretation is that the long-lived branch of $^{273}$Ds is associated with a structure different from that of the short-lived branch. \section{Summary and conclusions}

A beam-energy scan of the $^{238}$U($^{40}$Ar, $4n$--$5n$)$^{274,273}$Ds reaction was performed at SHANS-2. No correlated decay chain attributable to $^{273}$Ds was identified at excitation energies of 42 and 45 MeV, and upper limits of 0.34 and 1.09 pb were obtained. At 50 MeV, two complete decay chains of $^{273}$Ds were observed, and the extracted cross section of $0.14^{+0.18}_{-0.08}$ pb agrees with the value reported previously for the same reaction at DGFRS-2.

One of the observed chains follows the known high-energy, short-lived branch of $^{273}$Ds, whereas the other reveals a lower-energy, longer-lived branch. When the present data are combined with the literature results, the evidence supports the existence of two distinct decay branches in $^{273}$Ds, and the lower-energy branch is naturally interpreted as a candidate low-lying or isomeric state.

The daughter nucleus $^{269}$Hs shows a nonuniform population pattern. In addition to the previously recognized high-energy groups, the present work identifies a low-energy event at 8.915 MeV that is preferentially connected with the longer-lived branch of $^{273}$Ds. Although the present statistics remain limited, this behavior is compatible with the presence of an additional low-lying state in $^{269}$Hs.

The isotopic systematics of $Q_{\alpha}$, $T_{1/2}$, and reduced $\alpha$-decay width in the Ds and Hs chains provide further support for a structural change near $N = 162$. The overall picture is consistent with configuration-dependent $\alpha$ decay in odd-$N$ transfermium nuclei and provides new constraints on the ordering of low-lying quasineutron orbitals around the deformed $N = 162$ shell gap. More decisive conclusions will require higher-statistics direct-production measurements and dedicated structure calculations.
